# How it works

This page follows the data from the files in this repository to a loaded Rock database.

## The pipeline

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    cal[Calibration scripts] -->|run on Rock databases| raw[Raw exports<br/>local only]
    raw --> norm[normalize.js] --> src[Anonymous source ratios]
    src --> prof[Profile<br/>sample-church.json]
    cast[Cast households<br/>and groups] --> builder
    conf[Configuration] --> builder
    pool[Address pool] --> builder
    prof --> builder[Builder]
    builder --> pkg[Dataset package<br/>core-1.zip]
    pkg --> loader[Loader]
    loader -->|bulk copy| db[(Rock database)]
    db -->|same calibration scripts| check[validate.js]
    prof --> check
```

1. **Measure.** The calibration scripts run inside a Rock database and return only anonymous counts and ratios.
2. **Normalize.** `calibration/normalize.js` turns that output into a source file of ratios. Raw exports never leave the git-ignored `calibration/raw/` folder.
3. **Blend.** A profile (`calibration/profiles/sample-church.json`) blends one or more sources into target numbers, and records each exclusion and override with a reason. `resolve-profile.js` produces the final numbers.
4. **Pack.** The [builder](../builder/README.md) reads the Cast, the configuration, the address pool, the photos, and the resolved profile, and writes one package per dataset for one Rock major release, such as Rock 21 (decision 52). A package holds data only, never SQL, and no dates are fixed in it.
5. **Load.** The [loader](../loader/README.md) checks the package, that it is new enough for the package, and that the database's Rock is the package's major release, read from Rock's `Rock.Version.dll`. Then it generates the timeline for the install day, and writes the dataset into a fresh Rock database.
6. **Grade.** The loader CLI runs the same calibration scripts against the loaded database, and `validation/validate.js` compares the results with the profile.
7. **Verify.** Before a package is published, the [verify tool](../verify/README.md) loads it into a clean copy of the Rock release it is built on, the newest dot release of its major, and checks it against Rock itself, then writes a receipt. Publishing needs a passing receipt. See [Verification](verification.md) for every check and why it is there.

## One load, step by step

The loader runs the steps below in one database transaction. A failure rolls everything back and shows the error that stopped the load, even when the rollback fails too. `--dry-run` rolls back on purpose. Every inserted row carries `ForeignKey = SampleData:<dataset>`, except in the few Rock tables that have no such column (see [Loading a package](../loader/README.md#loading-a-package)).

1. **Configuration.** Connection statuses, service time slots, campuses and their schedules, funds with a child account per campus, and check-in rooms, with Student Night on Wednesday beside the Sunday student service. Check-in is then set up on purpose: the Weekly Service Check-in template's supervisor mode, checkout at the kiosk and in the Check-in Manager, and duplicate check-in prevention, Rock's next-gen labels on each area, room limits with firm ones in the nursery, a second room for Grades 4-6 with Rock's Balance strategy, and Rock's Test Group in every kids and student room at every campus on both of Rock's all-day test schedules. After serving is set up, a Volunteer Test Area under Rock's Volunteer Check-in template gets a test group at each campus in every room a team serves in, on the same test schedules, so volunteer check-in can be tested too while real teams keep only their real services (decisions 367 to 371). The Built on the Rock campaign fund gets the campaign's dates, and Rock's two pledge pages and default contribution statement point at it (see [Pledges](pledges.md)). It also sets up small groups: the capacity rule, the Host role, the group attributes, the topics, the inactive reasons, and a parent group per campus. Then it sets up serving: eight teams at every campus with their rooms, service times, staffing, and background check requirement, and Rock's Involvement connection type with the eight serving opportunities. Small group leaders get Rock's Background Check Required requirement too, which they cannot override (decision 334). Last, the church's own categories from `configuration/categories.json`, such as the Rock Solid Church data view category and its children, with their security (decisions 185 and 223), and the four person signal types from `configuration/signals.json`, with who may see and add each (decision 279).
2. **People.** Cast families, people, family memberships (including children shared between two homes), addresses with map points, and phones. Households at one address share one Location, as Rock saves them. Every address, and every campus's map point, carries the marks Rock leaves after its address service checks an address, so Rock never sends a sample address to one (decision 315). For Full 7 and Core 3, generated households are added from the profile, and their small groups are planned before they load. Every campus also gets businesses: the Cast's 6 on the main campus, and at least 2 generated ones at each other campus. Each business has one or two contacts, loaded as Rock's Business Contact relationship. Every other Cast known relationship loads too, both halves, such as Craig Lowe, who may check in his daughter Madison, and the Morgans, whose grandchildren Mason and Chloe Kelly show at a kiosk search for the Morgans. About 3% of attending generated families with a child 12 or younger get one adult from another family who may check the children in (decisions 365, 366, and 372). Cast staff join the security roles their stories give them, all roles Rock ships (decision 79). Each of the 23 staff gets the Staff person attribute category's values, such as title, department, supervisor, bio, and staff photo, which the Extended Attributes block on the person profile shows (decision 201), and joins the org chart: Rock's Organization Unit groups, each with its leader and staff (decision 202). Each campus's pastor is its leader in Rock, which Rock's campus page shows as Leader, so a baptism's follow-up task goes to them (decision 291). Alisha Marble loads into Rock's own admin person (Person Id 1) instead of a new person, so the `admin` login signs in as her (decision 177). A Cast person's allergy goes into Rock's Allergy person attribute, which check-in labels print (Alex Decker's peanut allergy). The loader fills in what Rock's save hooks would set: ages, grades, giving Ids, giving leaders, primary family, and campus.
3. **Photos.** Every Cast person gets their delivered profile photo, except the few left without one on purpose. Each serving opportunity gets its photo, and each campus its exterior photo, as the image of its Location (decision 317). In Core 3, about half of the generated households get faces from a shared pool, one photo ethnicity per family. Full 7's crowd gets none.
4. **Attendance.** For each Sunday in the history window (3 years by default), each household attends at its own rate, adjusted for holidays and the month. Adults are recorded at worship. Children check in to the room their age or grade puts them in on that date, with a security code per family per Sunday. A student who comes on their own gets their own code. Students in grades 7 to 12 also come on their own: an engaged student comes on some Sundays the family stayed home, and some keep coming for a while after the family stops. Generated adults aged 18 to 20 check in as students until they graduate. Students are planned after every family, from their own seed, so adults and young kids are not affected (decisions 146 to 148). Students also check in on Wednesday nights at Student Night: each Cast student with a Student Night habit, and generated regulars, mostly in weeks they also come on Sunday (decisions 363 and 364). Every check-in names a kiosk at its own campus, from `configuration/kiosks.json`: a family's usual kids kiosk, the student kiosk for a student on their own, and the volunteer kiosk for a volunteer. Each security code opens one check-in session at its kiosk, with the last four digits of a phone as the search (decisions 360 and 361). After their service, kids from the nursery through 6th grade are checked out by the adult who checked them in (decision 362). Last come the adults from other families: Karen Morgan brings her Kelly grandchildren on some Sundays their mother works, and a generated child's check-in adult checks them in on about a third of their family's Sundays, in that service. Grades 4-6 check-ins then alternate between its two rooms in arrival order. No attendance uses Rock's test schedules. Every attendance row, here and in small groups and serving, gets the check-in status Rock's save hook would give it: Checked Out for a kid's check-out, Present for any other check-in, and Not Present for a row with no present time (decision 260).
5. **Achievements.** The Kids Check-in streak type and its seven achievement types, from High Five to Century Club. Each child who checked in gets a streak, counting each week they checked in, Student Night included, and the achievement attempts Rock would have made at each of their check-ins. Next-gen check-in lists the seven types, so kiosks show each child's progress.
6. **Small groups.** The Cast's planned groups from `cast/groups` and the generated groups for each campus. About one in six of a campus's groups study the Women's Bible Study with a woman leader and only women, and one in ten the Men's Bible Study. No household is in a group before four weeks after it started coming, or after six weeks past its last Sunday. A third of the groups started on a recent launch Sunday, and two thirds of those are led by someone who was Ready to Lead six weeks before (see [Small groups](small-groups.md)). Each group gets its schedule, its meeting place at the host's home (or a meeting link), its members, its attribute values, and its history. Weekly attendance follows the church year in [Small groups](small-groups.md): fall and spring seasons, a light summer, and holiday weeks marked "did not meet". Each Cast group gets a photo of its leader and spouse, and Group Finder shows it on the group's card.
7. **Serving.** The Cast's rosters and generated volunteers at every campus, each with a rotation and preferences. Every Sunday in the history window, volunteers on their rotation serve when their family came and check in to serve; the next six weeks are scheduled ahead. A small group leader who launched from Ready to Lead was serving half a year before then, and never leads a team. See [Serving](serving.md).
8. **Connection requests.** A connected request behind every recent volunteer, people who just connected and wait as Pending, and active and inactive requests in the measured mix, each with its activity history. Team leaders connect them, except the main campus's new requests, which Alisha Marble connects (decision 178). Connectors record celebrations on some of the requests they have worked, the Cast's hand-written (decision 219). Due dates run by status, so each active request is due later, due this week, or past due by the mix in `configuration/connections.json` (decision 173).
9. **Background checks.** They follow the connection requests, as their own part (decision 333). From `configuration/background-checks.json`, each person gets one latest check, written the way Rock's Checkr provider writes it, with its report drawn as a PDF: a Cast story first, then every adult on the Kids Ministry and Student Ministry teams (a pass from the last few years), then small group leaders (85% current, 6% expiring, 5% expired, and 4% never checked), then anyone whose request to serve waits in the Background Check status (still in progress). Each finished check sets the person's Background Checked, date, and result, and every team member and leader gets the requirement result Rock's Calculate Group Requirements job would have saved. Ted Decker's check is too old, Bill Marble's runs out in about five weeks, Jerry Wright has none, and Frank Dexter's failed.
10. **Giving.** Each giving pattern produces gifts on its rhythm (weekly, bi-weekly, monthly, quarterly, irregular, occasional, year-end, or one-time). Gifts come through a channel: scheduled, online, in the offering, or a mailed check. They land in batches by day, campus, and channel, and checks always get batches of their own, because churches run them through a check scanner. Most couples give together as one giving unit, and about 10% give separately. Who pays how follows age and gift size, so older givers write most checks. Before the gifts are planned, the Built on the Rock pledges are planned: a pledger's usual gifts carry a campaign line sized to their pledge and how it is going, and some give extra campaign gifts. The pledges are written after the gifts, and both load only with the giving part. The fund's goal comes from the planned pledges and is written in every load (see [Pledges](pledges.md) and decision 126). Last come the refunds, at the calibrated rate of about 2.5 for every 1,000 gifts: duplicate online gifts, amounts typed with an extra zero, one fund of a two-fund gift, gifts from an earlier year, and checks the bank returned. Each is written the way Rock's refund saves it, in a " - Refund" batch. The Cast has two, Gabriel Hernandez's duplicate gift and Javelina Wash Coffee's returned check (see [Refunds](refunds.md)). A few gifts are uncommon: stock, vehicles, and equipment given in kind, with Rock's asset type and a description for the statement (see [Uncommon gifts](uncommon-gifts.md)).
11. **Giving analytics.** The loader fills in what Rock's Giving Automation job would have written if it had run every night: each giver's giving attributes, giving bin and percentile, and journey stage with a year of history. Rock's own stored procedures do part of this work. Each replayed run reads the gifts as they stood that night, so a gift refunded later still counts on the nights before its refund (decision 263).
12. **Giving alerts.** The sample church's three alert types (Large Gift, Smaller Than Usual, Late Gift) come with the 12 months of alerts Rock would have raised, as each night's run saw the gifts.
13. **Family analytics.** eRA, as if Rock's Family Analytics job had run every Tuesday at 8 PM since the start of the history window. A family enters when an adult gave four times in a year with one gift in the last six weeks, or the family came on eight of sixteen Sundays, and it exits after eight weeks with no gift, four with no Sunday, and fewer than eight Sundays in sixteen. Each member gets Currently an eRA, an eRA Start Date and End Date, eRA steps in Rock's Core Steps program, and eRA history lines. One more run at the start of install day takes in the newest Sunday, so the job's first run changes nothing. Then Rock's own giving and attendance count procedures fill Times Given, First and Last Checked-In, Times Checked-In, and First and Last Gave, and the job is turned on with Set Visit Dates off (decisions 323 to 326). It reads the rows the earlier steps wrote, so it always runs. See [eRA](steps.md#era).
14. **Prayer.** About 10 requests a week come in for every 1,000 adults who attended, gave, or were in a small group in the last year. Those adults ask most, and others on the church's records ask now and then, mostly once and a few several times, through the profile's months, weekdays, and hours. Each request gets a Rock prayer category by its weight in `configuration/prayer.json` and text built from that category's pool in `content/prayer`. A prayer administrator from the Cast approves most within hours, which sets them to expire 14 days later, and the newest few still wait for approval. The prayer team (the Cast's Prayer Team plus about 3% of active adults) prays through each approved request while it is active, each prayer a "Prayed" interaction, as Rock's prayer session records it. A few requests are flagged back to the approval queue, some get comments and a reply, and some older ones get an answer (decision 67).
15. **Notes.** The six note types on person records get their settings and View security (see [Configuration](../configuration/README.md#notes)). The Cast's hand-written notes from `cast/notes` load on every dataset. Generated people get notes built from the text pools in `content/notes`: who gets notes and how many follow the designed targets in the profile, and the day and time follow the measured shape. Some notes get replies from other staff, a few are alerts, private, or pinned, and some mention another staff member. Each mention in a note from the last 89 days leaves the mentioned staff member the notification message Rock makes. Their bell counts it and opens the note. A message from the last three days is unread, and every older one is read (decision 307). A load without Rock's DataEncryptionKey writes mentions as plain names and no messages.
16. **Reminders.** Reminders staff and leaders set for themselves, from `configuration/reminders.json`, in Rock's two shipped reminder types: a Personal Reminder about a person and a Group Reminder about a group. The Cast has three. Ted Decker's monthly check-in with Ashley Kelly, before her rent comes due, is due now. His reminder to ask Josh Sanders to share his story at the next baptism Sunday is upcoming. Bill Marble's reminder to plan the Marble Group's end-of-season party is upcoming too. On Core 3 and Full 7, staff also set reminders about some of the generated people they wrote notes on in the last year, and the leaders of some active generated small groups set one about their group. Each is dated from the install day, so some are complete, some are due, some are upcoming, and a few repeat. Last, each owner's reminder count, which Rock's bell shows, is set the way Rock's Process Reminders job sets it. See [How reminders load](../loader/README.md#how-reminders-load).
17. **Benevolence.** It follows the reminders, as its own part (decision 350). Rock's default type and three church types from `configuration/benevolence.json`, each locked to RSR - Benevolence and RSR - Rock Administration. Requests are saved the way Rock's request block saves them, with Dee Navarro as case worker: the Cast's four, such as Ashley Kelly's rent and the Wards' electric bill, and about 2 a year for every 1,000 generated adults, mostly approved, with results and amounts. Approved rent, utility, car, and medical requests have a signed assistance agreement drawn as a PDF. Each church type starts a workflow when a request is entered, which gives a request with no case worker to Dee and sends no email (decision 355).
18. **Steps.** Rock's Discipleship Path is built out to seven steps from `configuration/steps.json`, and every step comes from a record the earlier steps wrote (decision 86). Small Group, Serve, and Lead steps follow each small group and serving team membership, read back from the database. Give steps follow the giving journey, replayed every Tuesday night over the history window. Baptism steps follow the Baptism Date, which generated adults get here, with Baptized Here on the step and the person. Starting Point Class and Foundations fall on real class dates that count back from the install day: every volunteer took Starting Point before serving, a small group leader who launched from Ready to Lead and was not baptized by then took Foundations before that day, a few people per campus are in each current class, and the rest follow designed shares of active adults. The Student Path is a second program from the same file. Its steps follow the student check-ins the attendance step planned, and they are planned once in every load, right after attendance, so registrations read the same plan (decision 206): Student Camp and Mission Trip on church-wide event dates, Follow Jesus at camp or on a Sunday the student came, and Student Leader from August of junior or senior year to graduation, each for a designed share of the students who could. Many students who decide to follow Jesus are baptized on a later baptism Sunday, often the July one after camp, on the same Baptism step. People whose Complete steps cover every step type in a program get a completion for it, the way Rock's Update Step Program Completions job records it. Right after the steps, the staff follow-up workflows from `configuration/step-workflows.json` load, as part of the steps (decisions 290 to 292). Baptism Follow-Up starts when a Baptism step is completed, and goes to the campus pastor of the step's campus. Follow Jesus Follow-Up starts when a Follow Jesus step is completed, and goes to Isaac Silva, the Student Pastor. Volunteer Care Check-In is on the Serve step, and staff start it by hand. Each is a task with a form and no email. The workflow types were built 12 months before the install day, and every Baptism and Follow Jesus step completed since has the workflow Rock would have launched. A task whose step was completed in the last 14 days waits in its assignee's My Workflows list, and the assignee completed each older one. Josh Sanders's baptism task is complete, and Dennis Perry's is open (decision 293). See [Steps](steps.md).
19. **Assessments.** They follow the steps, as their own part. Rock's five assessments, DISC, Spiritual Gifts, Conflict Profile, Emotional Intelligence, and Motivators, are requested and taken by the rules in `configuration/assessments.json`: staff take all five when they start, Starting Point asks its class for Spiritual Gifts and Foundations for DISC and Motivators on the first night, every current leader takes DISC and Conflict Profile before leading, and some active adults take one on their own. Each person's answers lean toward a target and are scored with a port of Rock's own scoring, so the answers saved on each assessment and the results saved as person attributes always agree. Some requests stay pending, and each one's last reminder is where Rock's reminder job would have left it. The Cast's stories, including six DISC results taken outside Rock's own DISC, come from their household files. See [Assessments](assessments.md).
20. **Peer network.** Rock's Peer Network is on for small groups and serving teams. Campus pastors follow their campus's small group leaders, and staff follow up to 25 people in their care. Then each pair of people who shared a group, and each follow, gets the score Rock's Calculate Peer Network job's last two monthly runs would leave, with Rock's trend arrow: rising while two people stay, falling after one leaves, and flat for a follow (decisions 328 to 330). It reads the groups and notes the earlier steps wrote, so it always runs. See [Peer network](small-groups.md#peer-network).
21. **Data views.** The church's layered data views from `configuration/reporting.json`: foundations, the Ministry by Design stage views built from steps, next-step views filed by Ministry System, the communication flows' audiences with their email guard, the Safety views, the Finance views, and two Staff Workspace views built on tags, each stamped with the staff member who made it (decision 306). They load before communications, because the New Here and We Miss You group syncs are built on the foundations. A view that points at a registration view waits for step 23. Rock's own Foundational Views category is renamed Foundational Views (Shipped). A view that needs a part the load left out is skipped.
22. **Calendar.** The church's 51 events on Rock's Public and Internal calendars, with their audiences and photos. Each event gets an occurrence for each campus and start time, from the start of the history window to 13 months ahead, with its own schedule, place, and contact. Dates follow the rules steps and messages use, so Easter, the baptism Sundays, the classes, camp, and VBS fall on the same days everywhere. Rock's four sample events become church events in place. See [Calendar](calendar.md).
23. **Registrations.** Event registration setup for camp, the mission trip, the fall retreat, VBS, Parents' Night Out, Serve Day, Money Matters, Date Night, and the Women's Retreat: template categories, templates with forms, fees, discounts, and placements, and each year's instances linked to their calendar occurrence with a public name and URL slug, their linked group, and their placement groups. Seven registrations are open on every install day. Then the students who registered for camp, the mission trip, and the fall retreat, from the Student Path plan: last summer's campers are exactly the students with that camp's step, and the open ones fill in by how far into their window the install day is. Each registration has its registrar, fees, answers, and discount, payments in daily batches with payment plans and reminders, its people in the linked group, and campers on buses, in cabins, and in activities. Kids and adults register too, from the people and the small group and serving plans: families for VBS and Parents' Night Out, volunteers and their teens for Serve Day, couples for Date Night, and adults, couples, and friends from one small group for Money Matters and the Women's Retreat, with crews by grade, project teams, childcare per child, rooms, and the Allergy question saved to the child. Every camper and mission trip student has a release their registrar signed while registering, typed, with Rock's verification hash and a PDF drawn at install. Every registration has the notes and history Rock writes as it saves, instances whose reminder day has passed are marked sent when Rock's job would have sent them, and three data views about registrations sit under the church's Registrations category, one of them the mission trip's eligibility rule. See [Event registrations](registrations.md).
24. **Person attributes.** Rock's own person attributes, listed in `configuration/person-attributes.json`, which the Extended Attributes tab, check-in, and data views read. The Cast's values come only from their files: Employer, Position, Legal Notes, and Do Not Send Giving Statement. Generated people get drawn values, each from its own seeded stream, so no other row changes. About 10% of working-age adults have an employer, and every business contact works at their own business. Kids and students in half the households have a school, from invented schools near each campus. The kids of two single-parent households per campus have a pickup or custody note. Households that first came inside the history window have a Source of Visit and sometimes a Previous Church. A kid who never answered Allergy on a registration form gets the same draw the form uses. Everyone gets the values their records give. Some young kids, Cast or generated, have the ability level of the nursery or preschool room they check in to. Date Engaged, a person attribute the loader adds to Rock's Membership category, holds the day each Engaged or Leading adult first joined a small group or serving team or first gave, and a share of check writers have an Envelope Number. See [How person attributes load](../loader/README.md#how-person-attributes-load).
25. **Communications.** The church's texting numbers: a main line, a student line, and staff personal numbers, with one for each campus pastor. The branded "Rock Solid Church" email template with the approved logo, in Rock's email wizard, and its Legacy version for Rock's older editor (decision 269). Four email sections for Rock's newer email editor, styled like that template, in a Rock Solid Church category: Service Times, Upcoming Event, Give Online, and Built on the Rock Update (decision 301). A weekly newsletter list per campus, seven ministry and staff lists, and two care lists, New Here and We Miss You. Who is on each follows involvement: involved adults get their campus newsletter, Leading and Engaged adults opt in to more lists, and the Volunteers, Small Group Leaders, and Prayer Team lists copy the teams, the leaders, and the prayer team. ## Dates and repeatability

- Every date in a data file is relative: `yearsAgo` plus a month and day, or `daysAgo` for recent events.
- The **anchor** is the date everything counts back from. It defaults to today on the database server, the clock Rock Cleanup uses for ages (decision 152). Pass `--anchor yyyy-MM-dd` to pin it.
- Every random choice comes from a stream seeded by the `--seed` text and the household. The same seed and anchor give the same data, row for row.
- After install, Rock's own jobs move the data forward from the anchor, the way they do for any church. [After you install](after-install.md) says which jobs the load already covers, which to run, and which to watch.

## Where the loader goes

The loader CLI installs packages for developers and test harnesses. A block inside Rock calls the same loader library through the `SparkDevNetwork.Rock.SampleData.Loader` NuGet package, which builds for the .NET Framework Rock runs on. A person picks the parts to load, and the loader refuses a database that already has people (decisions 120 to 122). Later, configuration will save through Rock's own services. Rock will never depend on this repository; everything it needs arrives in the package.
