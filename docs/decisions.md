# Decisions

The decisions behind the sample data: what it contains, what patterns it follows, and why. This log covers strategy, not implementation. How the builder does its work lives in [How it works](how-it-works.md) and the [builder README](../builder/README.md).

Each row is the current decision. When a decision changes, update its row and its date, and say what changed. Add new decisions at the end of the right section. On a branch, a new decision takes a placeholder such as `78`, never a number; it gets its number when the branch merges (see [Working in parallel](maintaining.md#decision-numbers)).

This log wins over the [design spec](design-spec.md) wherever the two disagree.

## Time

| # | Date | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 2026-10-14 | Store who people are; generate what they did at install time, counting back from install day. Ages and grades never drift, and recent events (a first visit two weeks ago) stay recent. | Stored timelines go stale, and shifting fixed dates breaks Sundays, holidays, and paydays. |
| 152 | 2026-10-23 | Install day, the anchor a load counts back from, is today on the database server (SQL Server's `GETDATE`), not on the machine running the load. An explicit anchor still wins. The verify tool judges Rock Cleanup only while the server and the machine running Rock are both on the anchor's date, checked before and after the job; otherwise it fails rock-cleanup with the dates and says to run again. The release workflow sets its runner to Arizona time. | Rock Cleanup computes ages and daily counts from `GETDATE`, and Rock uses the machine's time zone when `OrgTimeZone` is `Local`. On GitHub's UTC runners, a release started in the afternoon crossed midnight mid-verify (v0.1.0-alpha.11) and reported a year of false age changes. One clock for the load and the referee, and a clear failure when that clock moves, keep a passing receipt meaningful. |

## Realism and calibration

| # | Date | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | 2026-10-15 | Calibration copies shapes and ratios from real churches, never their size or local setup. | The profile can absorb new sources as they arrive without taking on any one church's quirks. |
| 8 | 2026-10-15 | Never name or describe a calibration source anywhere. Raw measurements never leave the machine that ran them. | Churches shared their data in confidence, and the sample data ships with Rock. |
| 9 | 2026-10-15 | Distance targets are coarse, rounded values with no source recorded. A profile may take one section from one source. | Coarse targets are enough for placement and reveal nothing about where they came from. |
| 10 | 2026-10-15 | Realism is graded: the same measurements run against the generated data and must match the profile within tolerance. Anything we deliberately skip has a written reason. | Realism becomes a number, not an opinion. |
| 67 | 2026-10-21 | Prayer targets come from three places. The monthly pattern of requests is measured (source-1). Volume is designed: 500 requests a year per 1,000 active adults, about 10 a week, so prayer screens stay busy (changed from 250 on 2026-10-21). Volume is counted per active adult, but anyone on an active record can ask; adults with no attendance, giving, or group in the last year are about a third as likely to ask, the way people on a church's edge ask in a hard season. That keeps most people asking once. Categories, request length, the day and time requests arrive, and who asks are measured (source-2). The prayer team's activity is designed, not measured: nearly every request is approved within a day, about 95% are prayed for (6 times on average, within the first 2 weeks), and about 15% get a comment. A small team of about 3% of active adults does the praying, and the busiest tenth of them make about a third of the prayers. Every request carries its requester's campus, and a few are shared with a small group. Requests use the prayer categories Rock ships, with no new or renamed categories. Suicidal Tendencies gets no generated requests, and Current Events and Nation stay non-political. The Cast's Prayer Team prays and its Prayer Administrator approves in every dataset, and guests with no person record make about 6% of requests. Long requests stop near 500 characters. The Cast also has its own prayer requests, tied to their stories (cast/prayer-requests.json): together they cover every prayer screen state, 10 are always entered 1 to 5 days before install so even Core 1 and Core 3 show a full prayer list and prayer session, and every load checks each one's state. | A demo needs an active prayer team so every prayer screen has data, and measured team activity is too sparse to use. |
