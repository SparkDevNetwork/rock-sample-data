# Decisions

The decisions behind the sample data: what it contains, what patterns it follows, and why. This log covers strategy, not implementation. How the builder does its work lives in [How it works](how-it-works.md) and the [builder README](../builder/README.md).

Each row is the current decision. When a decision changes, update its row and its date, and say what changed. Add new decisions at the end of the right section. On a branch, a new decision takes a placeholder such as `78`, never a number; it gets its number when the branch merges (see [Working in parallel](maintaining.md#decision-numbers)).

This log wins over the [design spec](design-spec.md) wherever the two disagree.

## Scope and datasets

| # | Date | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2026-10-14 | Sample data installs into a fresh database only. | Simpler and much faster. Mixing sample data into real data is out of scope. |
| 2 | 2026-10-15 | There are three datasets: Core 1 (one campus), Core 3 (three campuses), and Full 7 (seven campuses and about 25,000 people). | Tests need speed, campus features need several campuses, and demos need volume. |
| 3 | 2026-10-15 | The Cast is the same in every dataset and always lives on the main campus. Extra campuses get their own people. | Tests and documentation can rely on the Cast no matter which dataset is installed. |
| 4 | 2026-10-16 | Core 3 gets 75 generated companion households at each of Peoria and Gilbert (it was 50). | Enough people to make campus filters and reports meaningful, while staying small. At 50, only about 16 households per campus still attend or give, too few to lead and fill each campus's 6 small groups (decision 55). |
| 5 | 2026-10-15 | Sample data is defined and built outside Rock. Rock installs a finished dataset and never depends on how it was made. | Church knowledge stays in data, and Rock stays free of sample data tooling. |
| 41 | 2026-10-16 | A dataset ships as a package of data, never SQL. One C# loader library installs packages; it is published to NuGet, and both a Rock block and a command-line loader for test harnesses use it. | One implementation for every way of installing, and a downloaded package can never run commands against a database. |
| 49 | 2026-10-16 | A dataset's manifest sets how many generated households each campus gets. Core 3 lists 75 at Peoria and 75 at Gilbert, and none at the main campus. `--crowd` still replaces it for speed tests. | Core 3 then matches decision 4 exactly, and the main campus holds only the Cast, as decision 3 promises. |
| 52 | 2026-10-16 | Each package is for one exact Rock. Its `package.json` records the Rock version and that Rock's newest migration, and the loader refuses a database whose newest migration differs. Development loads can override this with a warning; Rock's own installer cannot. Each Rock release gets its own packages. | The loader writes straight into Rock's tables, so a schema change can break a load partway. A Rock database stores no version number, but its newest migration identifies its schema exactly. |
| 66 | 2026-10-19 | A package is published only with a passing receipt from the [verify tool](../verify/README.md). The tool loads each package into a trusted base made from the same Rock release (Rock's migrations, start-up steps, and first-night post-install job, nothing else), checks the loader's recorded contract against Rock's schema, runs the invariants, runs Rock's own Rock Cleanup job as a referee, and checks the content standards. It runs when packages are made, never when a church installs one. | The loader skips Rock's business logic, so nothing in Rock says when the data is wrong. A receipt proves each exact package works on its exact Rock, and turns written rules into checks that fail. |

## Time

| # | Date | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 2026-10-14 | Store who people are; generate what they did at install time, counting back from install day. Ages and grades never drift, and recent events (a first visit two weeks ago) stay recent. | Stored timelines go stale, and shifting fixed dates breaks Sundays, holidays, and paydays. |

## Realism and calibration

| # | Date | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | 2026-10-15 | Calibration copies shapes and ratios from real churches, never their size or local setup. | The profile can absorb new sources as they arrive without taking on any one church's quirks. |
| 8 | 2026-10-15 | Never name or describe a calibration source anywhere. Raw measurements never leave the machine that ran them. | Churches shared their data in confidence, and the sample data ships with Rock. |
| 9 | 2026-10-15 | Distance targets are coarse, rounded values with no source recorded. A profile may take one section from one source. | Coarse targets are enough for placement and reveal nothing about where they came from. |
| 10 | 2026-10-15 | Realism is graded: the same measurements run against the generated data and must match the profile within tolerance. Anything we deliberately skip has a written reason. | Realism becomes a number, not an opinion. |
| 67 | 2026-10-21 | Prayer targets come from three places. The monthly pattern of requests is measured (source-1). Volume is designed: 500 requests a year per 1,000 active adults, about 10 a week, so prayer screens stay busy (changed from 250 on 2026-10-21). Volume is counted per active adult, but anyone on an active record can ask; adults with no attendance, giving, or group in the last year are about a third as likely to ask, the way people on a church's edge ask in a hard season. That keeps most people asking once. Categories, request length, the day and time requests arrive, and who asks are measured (source-2). The prayer team's activity is designed, not measured: nearly every request is approved within a day, about 95% are prayed for (6 times on average, within the first 2 weeks), and about 15% get a comment. A small team of about 3% of active adults does the praying, and the busiest tenth of them make about a third of the prayers. Every request carries its requester's campus, and a few are shared with a small group. Requests use the prayer categories Rock ships, with no new or renamed categories. Suicidal Tendencies gets no generated requests, and Current Events and Nation stay non-political. The Cast's Prayer Team prays and its Prayer Administrator approves in every dataset, and guests with no person record make about 6% of requests. Long requests stop near 500 characters. The Cast also has its own prayer requests, tied to their stories (cast/prayer-requests.json): together they cover every prayer screen state, 10 are always entered 1 to 5 days before install so even Core 1 and Core 3 show a full prayer list and prayer session, and every load checks each one's state. | A demo needs an active prayer team so every prayer screen has data, and measured team activity is too sparse to use. |

## People and the Cast

| # | Date | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | 2026-10-15 | Six connection statuses: Prospect, Participant, Known, Connected, Engaged, Leading. Rock's Visitor, Attendee, and Member are renamed; Prospect and Participant are kept; Leading is added. | A clear journey from first contact to leadership, and renaming keeps every Rock feature that relies on the built-in statuses working. |
| 12 | 2026-10-15 | Every person, church, and organization is fictional, including employers and businesses. | Real names invite objections and could point to real people. |
| 13 | 2026-10-15 | Phone numbers use the reserved 555-0100 to 555-0199 range, and emails use `example.org`. | Sample data must never reach a real person. |
| 14 | 2026-10-15 | The Cast covers every stage of life and every pattern the data supports: children of every age, teens, seniors, Participants, and every giving pattern. Today that is 87 households and 185 people. Names are chosen first, and photo ethnicity is assigned afterward, so the two stay independent. | The legacy Cast was mostly adults ages 20 to 58 and nearly all White. |
| 15 | 2026-10-15 | Every Cast household has a story and a test purpose. Featured families, the ones most used in testing, have a short bio for each person. | Testers and writers can find the right person for a case without reading data files. |
| 63 | 2026-10-19 | Every campus has businesses. The main campus has at least 6, and every other campus at least 2. The Cast holds the main campus's 6, each tied to a Cast story, so every dataset has them. The loader generates the rest. Full 7 follows the profile instead when that gives more: about 3% of giving units are businesses. A business's address is its work address. Generated businesses are small, often home-based, so they use pool addresses on residential streets. | Business Detail, business giving, and business contacts have data at every campus, in every dataset. |
| 64 | 2026-10-19 | Every business has one or two contacts, loaded both ways as Rock's Business Contact known relationship. A Cast business's contacts are Cast people who own or run it. A generated business takes an adult from a family on its campus, the most engaged first, and sometimes a married couple. No one is a contact for two generated businesses. | Rock's Business Detail lists the contacts, and each contact's Known Relationships shows the business. |
| 51 | 2026-10-16 | Children carry their family's connection status. A child is Engaged when an adult at home is in a small group or serving, or the family gives. | Rock sets a family's status on its children when the family is added, and young children cannot join a group or give on their own. |
| 78 | 2026-10-20 | People have notes, and the notes show what good use looks like, not the thin use most churches have today. Six note types: Rock's Personal, Pastoral, and Phone notes (Pastoral and Phone made selectable), plus new Follow-up, Counseling, and Safety Alert types. Every type has View security: staff for five, and pastoral workers only for Counseling. The Cast's notes are hand-written, tied to each household's story, and cover every state Rock shows: each type, alerts, private notes, pinned notes, replies, and mentions. Generated households get generated notes. Who gets notes, how many, and how often features are used are designed above the measured rates: for example, 75% of Known adults and 90% of Leading adults have a note in the last two years. Timing (workdays and office hours) and note length come from the measured source (source-3), with notes a little longer. Cast staff write the main campus's notes. Each other campus's oldest Leading adult writes as its campus pastor, and the church office writes every campus's phone notes and safety alerts. | A sample church should show a church what notes can do. Measured note use is sparse: churches keep few notes, mostly in one or two types, and rarely use alerts, replies, or mentions. |
| 79 | 2026-10-21 | The sample church uses only the security roles Rock ships, and the loader never creates one. Cast people join the roles their stories give them, mapped by Guid in `configuration/security-roles.json` (for example, Staff Users is RSR - Staff Workers, and Pastoral Staff is RSR - Pastoral Workers). Prayer Team is not a Rock role: it only marks the Cast's prayer team. RSR - Involvement Administration, which Rock 21 does not ship, is removed. Each campus pastor who writes notes joins RSR - Staff Workers and RSR - Pastoral Workers. A role name that is not mapped fails the Cast validator and the load. | Staff can sign in and see what their roles allow, such as the notes they write, and a church sees its own out-of-the-box roles at work instead of roles it does not have. |

## Attendance and giving

| # | Date | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 23 | 2026-10-15 | Adults are recorded as attending worship without checking in. Kids check in with a security code per family per Sunday. Attendance follows the measured holiday and month patterns. | This is how churches actually record each, so attendance reports and check-in screens both have real data. |
| 24 | 2026-10-15 | Giving has three channels: scheduled gifts (bank or card), one-time online gifts, and onsite gifts (check or cash) that only happen on Sundays the family attended. Year-end gifts are sized from the giver's normal giving. | Each channel shows up differently in Rock's finance screens and giving analytics. |
| 25 | 2026-10-15 | Each giver has one wallet: a card, a bank account, and a checkbook. Every gift's payment details agree with it, and check numbers count up per writer. | A person's gifts tell one consistent story across every screen. |
| 26 | 2026-10-15 | Check gifts come with scanned check images that match the gift exactly: the writer's name and address, the date, the amount, and the check number. Every check in Core 1 and Core 3 has images; Full 7 covers the last 12 months. Five fictional banks each have their own logo and paper color, ink is blue or black per writer, and routing numbers are invalid. Businesses that pay by check mail typed, signed checks. | Real-looking checks for finance screens and demos, with no real bank or account. |
| 27 | 2026-10-15 | A sample check scanned in a development Rock site finds its writer. | Check scanning can be tested end to end. |
| 42 | 2026-10-16 | Most married couples give together as one giving unit. About 10% give separately, each spouse a giving unit with their own pattern; in the Cast, the Sullivans, Tennants, Bennetts, and Silvas. Any other adult living with a married couple, such as an adult child at home, always gives on their own. | Rock's statements, giving analytics, and alerts are all per giving unit, and churches see both kinds of couples. |
| 43 | 2026-10-16 | Giving follows how real people give. Check writers lean older and give larger gifts; they bring checks on Sunday or mail them when they rarely come, and catch up after a missed Sunday. Large online gifts lean to bank transfers, and Apple Pay leans young. Hand-given gifts usually repeat the usual amount but stray now and then, schedules change a few times, and a few givers give exact amounts. Small givers give monthly or a few times a year instead of tiny weekly gifts, the top givers follow a long tail, and the largest give stock now and then. Givers start and stop, so about 30% lapse each year. | Rock's giving analytics, alerts, and finance screens behave the way they do at a real church. Validation passes 191 of 196 giving values. |
| 44 | 2026-10-16 | The loader fills Rock's giving analytics as if Rock's Giving Automation job had run every night: giving attributes as of each unit's last gift, bins and percentiles, and a year of weekly journey stage history. Where Rock has a stored procedure, the loader calls it; the rest is a line-for-line port of Rock's math. Rock's settings record the job's last run. | Giving screens, journey stages, and Rock's next job run all work on a fresh install, with Rock's exact numbers. |
| 45 | 2026-10-16 | The sample church has three giving alert types: Large Gift (gratitude, $5,000 or more), Smaller Than Usual (follow-up, 2 IQRs below the median, for givers whose median is at least $100), and Late Gift (follow-up, 3 standard deviations later than usual, for medians of at least $50). Each install comes with the 12 months of alerts Rock would have raised since the types were set up. | Rock ships no alert types. With these, the giving alerts list, its follow-up work, and alert history are ready to demo and test. |
| 46 | 2026-10-16 | A Cast giving pattern can say what Rock should show for that giver (frequency, journey stage, alerts), and every load checks it. Ted Decker gives by recurring credit card every month, so Rock shows him as a monthly, consistent giver. Margaret Carter, whose story already said she writes a check every month, is now the monthly check writer, with check images. The Cast has two lapsed givers: the Ortizes stopped about six months ago, and Emily Gray still attends but her scheduled card gift stopped seven months ago when her card expired. | Stories that documentation and tests rely on cannot quietly break when the generator or Rock changes. |
| 47 | 2026-10-16 | A lock file fingerprints the Rock code the builder ports, and a check script compares it with Rock on GitHub at a branch or release tag, never with a local checkout. Before each release, a parity test runs Rock's real Giving Automation job on loaded data and compares every giving value with the port. | The guard works the same on every developer's machine, and a Rock change cannot quietly make the sample data disagree with Rock. |
| 53 | 2026-10-16 | Each install has one Open batch of unmatched checks: the Sunday check batch at the main campus with the most checks in the last six weeks. Cast households with giving expectations keep their checks matched. The open checks have amounts, check numbers, images, and Rock's encrypted bank line fields, but no giver, and every writer's bank account is on file. The loader copies Rock's two-way encryption so Rock can read those fields. | Transaction matching can be tried on every fresh install, and Rock suggests the right giver for each check. The main campus holds the Cast, so the names are familiar. |
| 54 | 2026-10-16 | Checks are always in batches of their own, named for it. Each Sunday has two batches per campus, "Sunday Offering Checks" and "Sunday Offering Cash", and checks that come during the week land in "Mailed Checks". | Churches run checks through the check scanner, so checks are batched apart from cash and noted in the batch name. |
| 57 | 2026-10-19 | Kids check-in has one weekly streak type, Kids Check-in. It counts every check-in under Rock's Weekly Service Check-in template, from the nursery through grades 9-12, and starts on the first Sunday of the history. Every child who checked in has one streak. Attendance stays on, as Rock sets it for a new streak type, so check-ins after install keep the streaks current. Rock's own Weekly Attendance streak type and its two achievement types stay as Rock ships them. | One streak covers every child the way check-in records them. With attendance off, Rock would ignore new check-ins and the achievements would stop on install day. |
| 58 | 2026-10-19 | Kids Check-in has seven achievement types. High Five is 5 check-ins. Back-to-Back is 2 weeks in a row and Four-Peat is 4 in a row, and both can be earned again and again. Unstoppable is 10 weeks in a row. The 25 Club, Half Century (50), and Century Club (100) count check-ins in all; Half Century needs the 25 Club first, and Century Club needs Half Century. Every type but the two repeatable ones is earned once. None has a time limit or start date, and none counts past its goal. Week-in-a-row types show Rock's trophy, and the others its medal. | New kids get an early win, regulars keep winning short streaks, and long-time kids work toward the big ones. The chain shows Rock's prerequisites. |
| 59 | 2026-10-19 | Achievement attempts are what Rock would have left after processing each child's streak at every check-in, as of their last check-in. Streak counts are as of the anchor, because Rock's cleanup job refreshes them every day. Failed attempts stay, as they do in Rock, so Full 7 has about 183,000 attempts. | Rock processes achievements when a check-in saves a streak, and never again after a child stops coming. | 60 | 2026-10-19 | Check-in shows the achievements. The Weekly Service Check-in template's Achievement Types setting lists all seven. | Next-gen check-in shows each child's progress and new badges on its success screen and labels, so achievements can be demoed at a kiosk. |
| 61 | 2026-10-19 | A Cast person can say what Rock should show for their achievements, and every load checks it. Sam Lee has earned Century Club. Grace Fischer has Century Club in progress. | 65 | 2026-10-19 | Every business gives, and none has stopped: each has gifts in the last 12 months, and every load checks it. Generated businesses give monthly, bi-weekly, or irregularly, by card, bank transfer, or mailed check, from high on the giving curve but short of its very top. In Full 7, businesses are 3.2% of giving units and 6.8% of dollars (the profile has 3.1% and 8.4%). Generated businesses never give quarterly or once a year, because those givers are rare in the profile and big business gifts would skew them. ## Connections and serving

| # | Date | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 68 | 2026-10-19 | Requests to serve use Rock's Involvement connection type, and the teams people join are serving teams. Involvement keeps its name and Guid and gets a real description and icon.
